# Los Angeles: the case study memo

Screening research, not investment advice. Data through 2026-11-12; values as of 2026-11-12. Generated by `python -m mwall la` from the published outputs; every number below is a data cell or a cited source row.

## Summary

- The Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim metro holds 640 of the scored loans, $19.3B of whole-loan balance (6.2% of the national total).
- At today's terms, lenders would refinance $2.7B less than these loans owe, across 288 loans; in the downside, $5.1B. Office is 47% of the market's balance and $1.8B of its gap.
- 215 loans sit inside the City of Los Angeles, where Measure ULA taxes the whole price of a large sale: the modeled exit pays 5.5% for 117 of them and 4% for 17.
- Five case files, picked by a rule committed before any was drafted, show where the screen and simple rules agree, where they part, and where the data itself misleads.

### 1. Top office by opportunity score: Red Building

750 North San Vicente Boulevard, West Hollywood, CA. Highest opportunity score among the market's office loans.

A 411,547 square-foot office building from 2013, 71% occupied against 74% when the loan was securitized. Annualized NOI fell to $13.3M in the latest filing from $16.4M the month before, and the loan, interest-only at 4.39%, comes due on January 6, 2028. The maturity model puts the chance it fails to pay off then at 88% (90% interval 81% to 93%); among 1,843 office loans it ranks 102 on 12-month distress.

At the office debt-yield floor of 12.5%, a lender would size a new loan at $106.4M, leaving a refinance gap of $126.1M on a whole-loan balance of $232.5M. The engine values the building at $173.3M and bids $140.4M, 40% below the balance; in the downside the bid falls to $119.2M.

Local context: West Hollywood sits between two CBRE survey regions: West Los Angeles (22.6% vacancy, $5.57 a square foot a month) and the Hollywood/Wilshire Corridor (28.5%, $3.51). The filings don't say which one the building belongs to.

Risks and data gaps: The filing reports no NOI at securitization, so the cap rate is the national office benchmark of 8.82%, not a spread over this building's own appraisal; a 2013 West Hollywood building may trade tighter, which the cap-rate sensitivity on the tear sheet shows. Two of the three listed tenants, about 17% of the area, have leases ending in February 2029, after maturity. The whole-loan balance is estimated: this trust holds a $40.0M note of a larger pari passu loan.

### 2. Top multifamily by opportunity score: RESPARA

11610 DUNSTAN WAY, Los Angeles, CA. Highest opportunity score among the market's multifamily loans.

54 apartments built in 2022 in the City of Los Angeles, carrying $62.2M of whole-loan debt, $1.15M a unit. The five-year interest-only loan pays 6.30% and matures on February 11, 2029. Occupancy is 91% against 96% at securitization, and NOI of $4.3M runs below the $4.9M underwritten then. Its 12-month distress probability is 4.5%, rank 214 of 3,245 multifamily loans; the model's top drivers are the short loan term and the debt per unit.

At today's 6.65% refinance rate the debt service coverage test binds at $44.4M, a gap of $17.8M. The engine values the property at $70.0M and bids $55.9M, 10% below the balance ($50.1M in the downside). Selling in the City of Los Angeles adds Measure ULA: the modeled exit price clears the indexed upper threshold, so the sale pays 5.5% of the whole price on top of ordinary selling costs.

Local context: Los Angeles County multifamily vacancy is 5.4% (Kidder Mathews, CoStar data) and asking rents rose 0.6% in a year. Permits for units in buildings of five or more nearly doubled: 13,550 units authorized from January to September 2026 against 6,717 a year earlier (Census Bureau), supply that would arrive from about 2028.

Risks and data gaps: Built after October 1978 and less than 15 years old, the building falls under neither the City's rent ordinance nor AB 1482, so the model caps no rent growth; a new rent rule would cut the exit price. At $1.15M of debt a unit, there is little room if new supply lands nearby.

### 3. Top retail or industrial by opportunity score: LA Creative Industrial Portfolio

2079 East 15th Street, Los Angeles, CA. Highest opportunity score among the market's retail and industrial loans.

A 144,339 square-foot industrial property near downtown Los Angeles, built in 1923. Occupancy has fallen to 32% from 71% at securitization, annualized NOI to $249K, and reported debt service coverage to 0.39x. The servicer reports advances outstanding, yet the loan still files as performing. The interest-only loan pays 4.09% and matures on April 6, 2031. Its 12-month distress probability of 4.2% ranks 25 of 817 industrial loans.

On in-place income a lender would lend $2.4M against a balance of $15.2M: a gap of $12.8M. Valued on that income at the industrial benchmark (6.43%), the property is worth $8.6M, and the bid of $6.7M is 56% below the balance.

Local context: Los Angeles County industrial vacancy runs from 5.0% (CBRE) to 6.0% direct (Kidder Mathews). At 32% occupied, this building's trouble is its own, not the market's.

Risks and data gaps: Valuing a building this empty on in-place NOI understates what it would be worth leased: at the market's 95% occupancy its rent roll would be about 3.0 times today's, before the cost of releasing a building built in 1923. The filing names it a portfolio but files one property record, and NOI at securitization is reported as zero, so there is no appraisal spread to carry forward.

### 4. Model safe, debt-yield rule risky: SOS Storage Center

19840 Pioneer Avenue, Torrance, CA. Among performing loans with a debt yield under 8% (the rule of thumb's warning), the lowest 12-month distress probability.

A self-storage facility in Torrance, 801 units, built in 1992, 96% occupied in the latest report. The loan pays 7.58%, interest only, until September 6, 2033. Its 12-month distress probability is 0.6%.

The rule of thumb flagged a 2.1% debt yield. That figure was an error in the data: the latest filing reported three months of NOI ($299K) that the cleaning step read as a full year. With the year restored ($1.2M; decision D059), the debt yield is 8.5%. A refinance would still fall $2.7M short (a lender would lend $11.3M against $14.0M), but the bid of $14.6M is above the balance: a sale would repay the loan.

Local context: No public survey covers Los Angeles self-storage. Nationally, Public Storage guided 2026 same-store revenue to 0 to 0.5% and Extra Space Storage to 2.0 to 2.5%, which set the model's growth path for the type.

Risks and data gaps: The models still read the quarter as a year; their NOI inputs carry little weight, which is why the distress model rated the loan safe anyway. The lesson runs the other way too: a debt-yield screen on raw filings can be wrong for reasons that have nothing to do with the property.

### 5. Widest model and debt-yield disagreement: Holiday Inn Express Van Nuys

8244 Orion Avenue, Van Nuys, CA. The widest disagreement between the distress model's rank and the debt-yield rule's rank.

A 130-room hotel in Van Nuys, built in 1967, 83% occupied, with annualized NOI of $2.1M against a $10.0M loan: a debt yield of 21.4% and debt service coverage of 3.26x. The rule of thumb rates it among the safest loans in the market. The distress model ranks it 40 of 1,243 lodging loans (7.1%), driven by the loan rather than the hotel: a five-year term, a 6.47% coupon and a maturity 15 months away. The maturity model puts failure at 14%.

The hotel would refinance easily: no gap at today's terms, and a value of $21.1M gives a bid of $20.4M, about twice the balance. Lodging gets a direct-capitalization value only (confidence tier C).

Local context: Hotels in the Los Angeles market ran 75.9% occupancy at an average daily rate of $211 in the third quarter of 2026, with revenue per available room up 11.5% on the year (STR data via Cushman & Wakefield).

Risks and data gaps: Here the debt-yield rule looks right and the model's loan-term inputs over-flag: the model learned that short, high-coupon loans run into distress more often, but this property's coverage is far above any refinance test. One strong quarter is not a run rate, and a hotel built in 1967 invites capital spending the filings don't show.
