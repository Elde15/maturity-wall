# Los Angeles: the case study memo

Screening research, not investment advice. Bids are indicative economic values for the property, not offers for the loan. Data through 2026-11-12; values as of 2026-11-12. Every number below is a cell of the project's published outputs or a figure from a cited source.

## Summary

- The Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim metro holds 640 of the scored loans, $19.3B of whole-loan balance (6.2% of the $313.2B national total).
- By the model's estimate, these loans owe $2.7B more than lenders would refinance at today's terms, across 290 loans ($5.1B in the downside). Office is 47% of the market's balance and $1.8B of the gap. The gap measures how much new equity a refinance would need; it is not a forecast of defaults.
- 215 loans sit inside the City of Los Angeles, where Measure ULA taxes the whole price of a large sale: the modeled sale at the end of a five-year hold pays 5.5% for 115 of them and 4% for 16.
- Five case files show where the screen and simple rules agree, where they part, and where the data itself misleads.

## Three local rules

- Measure ULA: a sale inside the City of Los Angeles above $5.4 million pays 4% of the whole price, and 5.5% at $10.9 million or more (thresholds from July 1, 2026, indexed yearly), on top of the usual city and county transfer taxes. The model charges it on the modeled sale at the end of the hold: 115 loans at 5.5%, 16 at 4%.
- Proposition 13: a purchase resets the property tax to the price. The seller's tax is estimated from the appraisal at securitization grown 2% a year, capped at the tax on today's value (Proposition 8), and the buyer's tax follows its own price. Applied to 604 loans; 31 multi-property loans and 5 without an appraisal date are left out.
- Rent caps: 3% under the City's rent ordinance (21 loans), 1.919% in unincorporated Los Angeles County (2), and the state's 8.7% (33). Against the model's 2.0% growth path only caps below it change the cash flows: 0, 2 and 0 loans respectively. The two local ordinances also rule out the renovation program.

## Case files

### 1. Top office by review priority: Red Building

750 North San Vicente Boulevard, West Hollywood, CA. Highest review priority among the market's office loans; it ranks high on loan size, discount to the balance and refinance gap.

A 411,547 square-foot office building from 2013, 71% occupied against 74% when the loan was securitized. Its latest NOI, $13.3M a year, is annualized from the first six months of 2026; the filing before reported $16.4M for a full year, so part of the drop may be seasonal. The loan, interest-only at 4.39%, comes due on January 6, 2028. The maturity model ranks it 90 of 529 office loans near maturity (estimate 88%, 90% interval 81% to 93%); the rank is the more reliable reading.

At the office debt-yield floor of 12.5%, a lender would size a new loan at $106.4M, leaving a refinance gap of $126.1M on a whole-loan balance of $232.5M. Capitalizing $13.3M of NOI at 8.83% gives a value of $150.7M. The bid is $120.7M, 48% below the balance; in the downside it falls to $97.9M.

Local context: West Hollywood sits between two CBRE survey regions: West Los Angeles (22.6% vacancy, $5.57 a square foot a month) and the Hollywood/Wilshire Corridor (28.5%, $3.51). The filings don't say which one the building belongs to.

What would make this wrong: The filing reports no NOI at securitization, so the cap rate is the national office benchmark, not a spread over this building's own appraisal; a 2013 West Hollywood building may trade tighter, which would raise the value (the tear sheet shows the cap-rate sensitivity). Two of the three listed tenants, about 17% of the area, have leases ending in February 2029: the return bid already charges for re-leasing that space in year three, while the value bid doesn't, one reason the return bid is the lower of the two. The whole-loan balance is estimated: this trust holds a $40.0M note of a larger loan split across trusts (pari passu).

Sources: CBRE, 2026-10-09 (https://mktgdocs.cbre.com/2299/7ec14828-8427-4620-9ede-86f2b7e247cf-790511792/Q3_2026_Los_Angeles_Office_Fig.pdf)

Tear sheet: search "Red Building" on the site's Loans page.

### 2. Top multifamily by review priority: RESPARA

11610 DUNSTAN WAY, Los Angeles, CA. Highest review priority among the market's multifamily loans; it ranks high on confidence tier, loan size and discount to the balance, while its own risk ranks lower.

54 apartments built in 2022 in the City of Los Angeles, carrying $62.2M of whole-loan debt, $1.15M a unit. The five-year interest-only loan pays 6.30% and matures on February 11, 2029. Occupancy is 91% against 96% at securitization, and NOI of $4.3M runs below the $4.9M underwritten then. Its 12-month distress probability of 4.5% ranks 214 of 3,245 multifamily loans; the model's top drivers are the short loan term and the debt per unit.

At today's 6.65% refinance rate the debt service coverage test binds at $44.4M, a gap of $17.8M. Capitalizing $4.3M of NOI at 6.59% gives a value of $64.9M, and the bid is $51.8M, 17% below the balance ($45.3M in the downside). A sale in the City of Los Angeles pays Measure ULA: the modeled sale at the end of a five-year hold clears the indexed upper threshold, so it pays 5.5% of the whole price on top of ordinary selling costs.

Local context: Los Angeles County multifamily vacancy is 5.4% (Kidder Mathews, CoStar data) and asking rents rose 0.6% in a year. Permits for units in buildings of five or more nearly doubled: 13,550 units authorized from January to September 2026 against 6,717 a year earlier (Census Bureau), supply that would arrive from about 2028.

What would make this wrong: Built after October 1978 and less than 15 years old, the building falls under neither the City's rent ordinance nor the state cap, so the model caps no rent growth; a new rent rule would lower the sale price. At $1.15M of debt a unit, there is little room if new supply lands nearby.

Sources: Kidder Mathews, 2026-10-07 (https://kidder.com/wp-content/uploads/market_report/multifamily-market-research-los-angeles-2026-3q.pdf); U.S. Census Bureau, Building Permits Survey, 2026-10-27 (https://www2.census.gov/econ/bps/County/co2609y.txt); U.S. Census Bureau, Building Permits Survey, 2026-01-13 (https://www2.census.gov/econ/bps/County/co2509y.txt)

Tear sheet: search "RESPARA" on the site's Loans page.

### 3. Top retail or industrial by review priority: LA Creative Industrial Portfolio

2079 East 15th Street, Los Angeles, CA. Highest review priority among the market's retail and industrial loans; it ranks high on confidence tier, refinance gap and discount to the balance.

A 144,339 square-foot industrial property near downtown Los Angeles, built in 1923. Occupancy has fallen to 32% from 71% at securitization, annualized NOI to $249K, and reported debt service coverage to 0.39x. The servicer reports advances outstanding, yet the loan still files as performing. The interest-only loan pays 4.10% and matures on April 6, 2031. Its 12-month distress probability of 4.2% looks low for these facts, but it ranks 25 of 817 industrial loans, in the riskiest 3%; the rank is the more reliable reading.

On in-place income a lender would lend $2.4M against a balance of $15.3M: a gap of $12.8M. Capitalizing $249K of NOI at the industrial benchmark of 6.43% gives a value of $3.9M, and the bid of $3.0M is 81% below the balance.

Local context: Los Angeles County industrial vacancy runs from 4.3% (Cushman & Wakefield) to 6.0% direct (Kidder Mathews), with CBRE at 5.0%. At 32% occupied, this building's trouble is its own, not the market's.

What would make this wrong: Valuing a building this empty on in-place NOI understates what it would be worth leased: at the market's 95% occupancy its rent roll would be about 3.0 times today's, before the cost of releasing a building built in 1923. The filing names it a portfolio but files one property record, and NOI at securitization is reported as zero, so there is no appraisal spread to carry forward.

Sources: Cushman & Wakefield, 2026-10-15 (https://www.cushmanwakefield.com/en/united-states/insights/us-marketbeats/greater-los-angeles-marketbeats/industrial); CBRE, 2026-10-09 (https://mktgdocs.cbre.com/2299/f4c9f94f-fac8-4789-963f-04ce5ded29d4-2121070221/Los_Angeles_Industrial_Figures.pdf); Kidder Mathews, 2026-10-02 (https://kidder.com/wp-content/uploads/market_report/industrial-market-research-los-angeles-2026-3q.pdf)

Tear sheet: search "LA Creative Industrial Portfolio" on the site's Loans page.

### 4. Model safe, debt-yield rule risky: SOS Storage Center

19840 Pioneer Avenue, Torrance, CA. Among performing loans with a debt yield under 8% (the debt-yield rule at 8%), the lowest 12-month distress probability.

A self-storage facility in Torrance, 801 units, built in 1992, 96% occupied in the latest report. The loan pays 7.58%, interest only, until September 6, 2033. Its 12-month distress probability is 0.6%.

The rule flagged a 2.1% debt yield. That figure was an error in the data: the latest filing reported three months of NOI ($299K) that the cleaning step read as a full year. A later fix (decision D059) restored the year in the underwriting inputs ($1.2M, a debt yield of 8.5%) but not in the models' inputs, which the rule also reads, so the rule still flags this loan and the models still see the quarter. On the restored NOI, a lender would lend $11.3M against $14.0M, a gap of $2.7M. The bid is $13.6M; a sale at that bid would fall $412K short of the $14.0M balance.

Local context: No public survey covers Los Angeles self-storage. Nationally, Public Storage guided 2026 same-store revenue to 0 to 0.5% and Extra Space Storage to 2.0 to 2.5%, which set the model's growth path for the type.

What would make this wrong: Its confidence tier is B rather than the A that self-storage normally gets, because the NOI was annualized from fewer than six months. The distress model rated the loan safe even on the bad NOI, because its NOI inputs carry little weight. A debt-yield screen on raw filings can be wrong for reasons that have nothing to do with the property.

Sources: Extra Space Storage (PR Newswire release, finviz copy), 2026-10-27 (https://finviz.com/news/409291/extra-space-storage-inc-reports-2026-third-quarter-results); Public Storage, 2026-10-26 (https://s1.q4cdn.com/588671402/files/content_files/20261026-PUBLIC-STORAGE-REPORTS-THIRD-QUARTER-2026-RESULTS-AND-RAISES-GUIDANCE-FINAL.pdf)

Tear sheet: search "SOS Storage Center" on the site's Loans page.

### 5. Widest model and debt-yield disagreement: Holiday Inn Express Van Nuys

8244 Orion Avenue, Van Nuys, CA. The widest disagreement between the distress model's rank and the debt-yield rule's rank.

A 130-room hotel in Van Nuys, built in 1967, 83% occupied, with annualized NOI of $2.1M against a $10.0M loan: a debt yield of 21.4% and debt service coverage of 3.26x. The debt-yield rule rates it among the safest loans in the market. The distress model ranks it 40 of 1,243 lodging loans (7.1% probability), driven by the loan rather than the hotel: a five-year term, a 6.47% coupon and a maturity 15 months away. The maturity model's estimate is 14%.

At today's terms the engine finds no refinance gap. Lodging gets a value bid only (confidence tier C, no cash-flow model): capitalizing $2.1M of NOI at 10.11%, net of the buyer's property tax reset under Proposition 13, gives a value of $21.1M and a bid of $20.4M; a sale at that bid would cover the $10.0M balance.

Local context: Hotels in the Los Angeles market ran 75.9% occupancy at an average daily rate of $211 in the third quarter of 2026, with revenue per available room up 11.5% on the year (STR data via Cushman & Wakefield).

What would make this wrong: Here the debt-yield rule looks right and the model's loan-term inputs over-flag: the model learned that short, high-coupon loans run into distress more often, but this property's coverage is far above any refinance test. One strong quarter is not a run rate, and a hotel built in 1967 may need capital spending the filings don't show.

Sources: Cushman & Wakefield (STR/CoStar data), 2026-10-27 (https://content.cushmanwakefield.com/api/public/content/5671dca76e72465aae18e8831c38fc5d?v=ef351935)

Tear sheet: search "Holiday Inn Express Van Nuys" on the site's Loans page.

## What the five cases show

- Red Building: Model and rules agree: a half-empty office building near maturity at today's rates.
- RESPARA: A new building can be a refinance problem without being a credit problem: the gap comes from rates and the debt per unit, not from the property.
- LA Creative Industrial Portfolio: The model's probability level understates trouble that its rank and the filings make plain.
- SOS Storage Center: The data itself misled: a quarter's NOI read as a year made a sound loan look dangerous to the rule.
- Holiday Inn Express Van Nuys: Where the model and the rule part, the filings side with the rule: the property covers its debt three times over, and the model is reacting to the loan's terms.

## Method and limits

The selection rule and the full candidate list (https://github.com/Elde15/maturity-wall/blob/main/docs/la/selection.md and https://github.com/Elde15/maturity-wall/blob/main/docs/la/candidates.csv) were committed to the repository (b522998 on 2026-11-16) before any case file was drafted. Two fixes came after the selection was committed: three-month NOI that had been read as a full year was corrected in the underwriting inputs (decision D059), and the seller's estimated property tax was capped at the tax on current value (Proposition 8, decision D064). Rerun on the corrected outputs, the rule picks the same loans in four of five slots; for retail or industrial it now picks Vernon Industrial (review priority 82.4, against 80.9 for the committed pick, LA Creative Industrial Portfolio). The Proposition 8 cap lowered Vernon Industrial's estimated value and put its bid below the balance, which raised its discount and gap ranks. The case files keep the committed picks: swapping one after seeing the results would undo the point of writing the rule down first. The decisions are in https://github.com/Elde15/maturity-wall/blob/main/docs/decisions.md.

Local market figures name their publisher, because brokerages measure different building universes and their figures for one market can differ by several points.
