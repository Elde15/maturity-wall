## What the results say

- The distress model ranks loans well out of time and out of loan. In 2025 the riskiest 10% of loan-months held 28% of the distress events and 37% of the distress balance. Whole deal families held out cost it at most 0.007 PR-AUC.
- Distress in the next 12 months and failure at maturity are different events here. The distress score doesn't rank maturity failure (ROC 0.51), and the refinance gap barely did either: 44% of loans with a gap failed against 42% without one. Property type and servicing status say more, in a sample of 431 maturities.
- Market inputs matter and current NOI adds little: dropping market inputs cut 2025 PR-AUC from 0.081 to 0.043; dropping every NOI input cut it by 0.003.
- External check: the delinquency stock in this data tracks Trepp's published rate with a 0.97 correlation over 94 months, at a lower level because the universe is 2017-and-later deals. The 1,494 published figures were quote-checked against saved pages (`config/research/cmbs_delinquency_sources.csv`).

## Limitations added at this gate

1. Probability levels drift between years (checklist item 3). Rankings are the reliable output.
2. The distress model carries almost no rate channel; the maturity model and the refinance gap carry it.
3. The maturity model rests on 431 loans; its intervals average 26 points wide, and with 60 or 120 days' grace instead of 90 it no longer beats the best rule on Brier (0.295 against 0.294; 0.294 against 0.289), though it still ranks better.
4. 580 loans in the latest month have no usable payment status and are not scored (D055).
