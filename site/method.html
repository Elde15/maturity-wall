Method

How the numbers are made, and where they fail

Everything here is rebuilt from public filings and published market data. The models were tested on years and loans they never saw, against the rules of thumb a lender would use. Where they fell short, it says so.

  • Rankings held up; probability levels didn't. In every test year the models ranked risky loans ahead of the rules, but their probability levels drifted from year to year. Read a probability with its rank and, where one is given, its interval.
  • The refinance gap measures exposure, not default. It is the new equity a loan would need to refinance at today's terms. On its own it separated the loans that failed at maturity only weakly.
  • Bids are estimates for the property, not offers for the loan. They follow from stated assumptions, each cited below.

01Data

Loan-level filings of registered CMBS trusts

Since 2017, every SEC-registered CMBS trust files a monthly Form ABS-EE whose exhibit EX-102 lists each loan and property: balance, coupon, maturity, payment status, NOI, DSCR, occupancy, appraisal, the three largest tenants and servicer actions. The project found 381 such trusts by checking which filers' exhibits use the SEC's CMBS loan template, rather than guessing from trust names, and parsed 23,970 filings with its own parser, and rebuilt each loan across its monthly reports.

A loan is not a row. Filers use at least six layouts, and in one of them every property of a 39-property loan repeats the full loan balance. Large loans are split into pieces of equal rank (pari passu notes) held by different trusts; property NOI covers the whole loan while each trust reports only its piece, so every ratio here uses an estimated whole-loan balance, and pieces of one loan are counted once. Property addresses get a county and metro from the Census geocoder and published crosswalks, never from a ZIP prefix.

The scored set is each loan's latest monthly report: 13,047 loans in 376 trusts that still report, with of estimated whole-loan balance.

Pool balances tie to the trusts' monthly distribution statements (Form 10-D) within 0.5% in 20,377 of 22,863 trust-months; most of the rest trace to a named defect in the filings. Every cleaning choice is logged in the project's decision log.

02Definitions

What each number means

The ratios

NOI
Net operating income: a property's rent and other income less its operating expenses, before loan payments and capital spending. Filings report it for a year or annualize part of one.
Cap rate
NOI divided by value. A higher cap rate means a lower value for the same income.
LTV
Loan to value: the loan balance over the property's value.
DSCR
Debt service coverage ratio: NOI over the year's loan payments. At 1.25x, income covers the payments 1.25 times.
Debt yield
NOI over the loan balance. It ignores the interest rate and the amortization, so lenders use it as a floor that low rates can't stretch.

The project's measures

12-month distress
For a loan performing today (current or under 30 days late, not in special servicing, not past maturity): within 12 months it goes 60 or more days delinquent, transfers to special servicing, is modified, fails to pay at maturity, or is liquidated.
Failure at maturity
The loan is not paid in full within 90 days after its maturity date. The maturity model scores it for loans 2.5 to 24 months from maturity; closer than 2.5 months, the newest filing can be older than the time left, so the model stops there (a design choice, not a tested cutoff).
Refinance gap
What a loan owes beyond the largest new loan a lender would make today: the smallest of the LTV, DSCR and debt yield limits, at the 10-year Treasury plus CBRE's lending spread (terms under Underwriting). It is the equity a refinance would need, not a forecast of default.
Value
NOI after the scenario's haircut (none in Base), divided by a cap rate set relative to the market. Example: an office property appraised at a 6.0% cap rate when the office survey stood at 6.5% carries a spread of -0.5 points; with the survey at 8.0% today, its cap rate is 7.5%. Spreads are held between the 10th and 90th percentile for the type. With no NOI or appraisal at securitization there is no spread, and the cap rate is today's benchmark for the type ( loans). Types without a survey series of their own (lodging, self-storage, mixed use and other) use a composite of the others ( loans).
Value bid
Value less a year of capital reserves, the cost of leasing listed tenant space that rolls within a year, and the buyer's closing costs.
Return bid
The highest price at which a buyer financed with a bridge loan still earns the hurdle, a 20% levered internal rate of return (IRR), over a five-year hold. It charges for leases that roll during the hold.
Implied bid
The lower of the value bid and the return bid.
Discount
One minus the implied bid over the whole-loan balance; zero when the bid is above the balance. At a bid at or above the balance, a sale at the bid would cover the loan.
Confidence tier
It starts from the type: A for multifamily, industrial and self-storage; B for office and retail (rollover is lease by lease and only three tenants are listed); C for lodging, mixed use and other, which get a value bid only. NOI older than 18 months, or annualized from fewer than six months, drops one tier. An A or B loan with no square feet or unit count, needed for reserves and leasing costs, drops to C. NOI that is zero, negative or older than 36 months isn't used: no tier and no value.
Review priority
A 0 to 100 score that orders loans for a closer look (defined under Underwriting). It is not a recommendation.
03Models

Tested out of time, out of loan, against simple rules

The 12-month distress model is a logistic regression on 63 loan, property, servicing and market inputs plus property type and payment status, with its credit signs held to credit logic: more debt yield, DSCR or occupancy can't raise risk, and more leverage or a higher refinance rate can't lower it. The maturity model, which estimates failure at maturity, is a logistic regression on 18 inputs, including the refinance gap and a rate-shock measure, fit on the 431 loans that reached maturity inside the data (173 failed), with the same sign rule. Those final models score today's loans; the tests below used earlier versions fit on less data.

Each test year starts on January 1, called D. Its model was fit only on information public by D:

  1. Trainloan-months ending about 25 months before D
  2. Tune and calibratethe next 12 months, whose 12-month outcomes were public by D
  3. DJanuary 1 of the test year
  4. Testthe 12 months from D

Whole loans were also split into five groups, and each group was scored by models that never saw it. The baselines are the rules a lender would reach for: the base rate (every loan gets the average event rate), debt yield under 7, 8, 9 and 10% and as a continuous rank, DSCR under 1.00, 1.10 and 1.25, and a positive refinance gap. The fixed thresholds scored close to one another, so the table shows debt yield under 10%, DSCR under 1.25 and the rank. The maturity tests use debt yield under 8% and DSCR under 1.25, and the Los Angeles selection rule uses the same 8%.

Where the models fail

  • Probability levels drift between years. The calibration slope was inside the 0.8 to 1.2 band only in 2023. In 2022 the distress model forecast 0.2% on average against a realized 1.3%, because it had been calibrated on loan-months from late 2019 to late 2020, the first COVID year. The ranking held up every year; the level didn't. So the site gives each probability with the loan's rank within its type and, for the maturity model, a 90% interval, and the case files lead with the rank.
  • The distress model carries almost no rate channel. In the training years distress came mostly from 2020, when rates fell, so the sign rule holds the rate inputs near zero. Rate risk lives in the refinance gap and the maturity model.
  • The maturity model rests on 431 loans. Its 90% intervals average 26 points wide, and property type does much of the work.
  • The refinance gap explained little of who failed at maturity. The gap is still the headline because it measures something else: how much new equity owners would need to refinance at today's terms. Whether a loan fails turns on whether someone puts that equity in, through reserves, the sponsor, an extension or a sale. A large gap means a loan needs a fresh decision from its owner and lender; the maturity model, which weighs the gap with leverage, type and other inputs, is the better guide to which loans fail.
  • Coverage is not the market. Registered conduit and single-borrower CMBS since 2017 is a slice of commercial real estate debt, and most 2016 deals, the source of the 2026 ten-year maturities, filed no loan-level data. Delinquency in this data tracks Trepp's published rate (correlation 0.97 over 94 months) at a lower level, 2.5% against Trepp's 5.2% on average, because Trepp's universe includes older deals.
04Underwriting

From a loan to a bid

For each loan with usable NOI the engine asks what a lender would lend today, what the property is worth, what a buyer could pay for it, and what that buyer earns over five years. The answer is an indicative economic bid for the property. A loan inside a CMBS trust is controlled by its special servicer, and buyers reach the asset by buying the note, buying the property after foreclosure, negotiating a discounted payoff or recapitalizing the sponsor; the bid is not an executable offer.

  1. Value. In-place NOI, less the scenario's haircut, over a relative-value cap rate. In Los Angeles and Orange counties a sale resets the property tax to the price (Proposition 13). The buyer's tax depends on the price and the price on the tax, so the engine solves both together. The seller's tax isn't filed; it is estimated from the appraisal at securitization grown 2% a year and capped at the tax on today's value, because an assessment can't exceed market value (Proposition 8). A buyer paying more than the old assessment pays more tax, which lowers value; for a property now worth less than its old assessment, a sale changes the tax little.
  2. Refinance test. The smallest of LTV, DSCR and debt yield sizing on the terms below, with DSCR measured on an amortizing payment; the gap is the balance above it.
  3. Bids. The value bid, and the return bid: the highest price at which the buyer still earns the hurdle.
  4. Cash flows. Five annual periods: NOI growth by type and metro, rent caps where local law sets them, lease rollover with renewal odds, downtime and leasing costs for office, retail and industrial, reserves, a bridge loan at the 5-year Treasury plus a spread, and a sale at the going-in cap rate plus 50 bp less selling costs and city transfer taxes (Measure ULA in the City of Los Angeles).

Today's refinance terms

Three loans were rebuilt in an Excel workbook with every step as a formula; the engine matches it to the cent, and the JavaScript engine running the scenario lab matches the Python engine to the cent on 50 random loans. That checks the arithmetic, not the assumptions: a wrong cap rate still gives a wrong bid, computed exactly. Across about 1.3 million steps of a rate and cap-rate grid, no gap shrank as rates rose and no value or bid rose with cap rates.

Review priority

Review priority (the project plan calls it the opportunity score) averages six percentile ranks, each from 0 to 1, and scales the result to 0 to 100. Each rank points the same way, toward a loan worth a closer look: higher risk (1 for a loan in distress now, else its maturity-model or distress probability), a larger refinance gap as a share of the balance, a larger discount of the bid to the balance, a higher levered IRR to a buyer paying the value bid, a higher confidence tier (A 1, B 0.5, C 0), and a larger balance. The weights are equal because no weighting was tested against outcomes. It orders loans for review and says nothing about whether to buy.

How the return bid is found: the buyer's net present value at the hurdle rate is a straight line in the price on each side of the price where the loan-to-value limit starts to bind, so the engine solves it exactly on each side instead of searching.

06Limitations

What the numbers can't see

  • Appraisal cap rates and broker-survey cap rates aren't the same measure: appraisals at securitization run below the survey (median office spread about -0.7 points). Carrying that spread forward gives lower cap rates, and so higher values and bids, than the survey alone would; Los Angeles appraisals run richer still. Every bid is shown with a cap-rate sensitivity.
  • Only three tenants are listed per property, with no rents; rent is spread evenly over occupied space.
  • California property taxes use an estimated seller's tax (the securitization appraisal grown 2% a year, capped at the tax on today's value) and county average rates.
  • An extension counts as a failure. The maturity outcome uses the maturity date first filed, so a loan extended past it, by an option or a modification, counts as not paid on time, though a lender may see an exercised extension option as routine.
  • Every refinance is sized as a new fixed-rate conduit loan, whatever the existing loan's rate type; a floating-rate loan's rate cap and extension tests aren't modeled. DSCR sizing uses an amortizing payment, stricter than the interest-only payment many of these loans pay today.
  • Defeased loans are left out: their collateral is Treasury securities, not the property.
  • The weakest assumptions: office downtime comes from a software vendor's leasing primer, not a survey; renewal odds come from one REIT's disclosures; buyer closing costs from one worked example; and reserves from a broker's glossary. Each is cited in the parameter table.
  • Reported NOI is often months old and sometimes annualized from part of a year; older or partial NOI lowers the confidence tier.
  • Multi-property loans are valued on pooled NOI, without listed-tenant rollover or local rules.
  • Nothing here knows a property's condition, its ground lease, the loan's own reserves, or who is willing to bid.
